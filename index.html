Welcome

This tool helps employees estimate pay and benefit costs before finalizing enrollment decisions. The tool also highlights the County’s investment in employee benefits.

Before You Begin

Have your annual salary or hourly rate available. This tool estimates gross pay and selected deductions only. It does not calculate taxes or final payroll amounts.

How to Use This Tool

  1. Begin with the Salary/Hourly Pay tab and enter your annual salary or hourly rate.
  2. Enter your date of birth on the Salary/Hourly Pay tab. Your date of birth is used to calculate age-based benefit estimates.
  3. Review the Healthcare Plan Comparison for factual differences between the available medical plans.
  4. Use Healthcare Elections to select the benefits you would like to estimate.
  5. Review your VRS retirement plan and any applicable voluntary retirement contributions.
  6. Use Paycheck Impact to review estimated deductions and County-paid contributions.
  7. Review the Summary tab for an overall estimate of your selections.
  8. Use Benefits Education for additional factual information about the benefit options included in the calculator.

Disclaimer

The calculator presents factual information to help employees understand available options. It does not recommend a plan, contribution amount, or benefit election. Employees should review official plan documents and consult personal advisors as appropriate.

Benefits Education

Understanding the HSA Plan and Traditional Plan

The HSA Plan has lower employee premiums and higher deductible exposure. The Traditional Plan has higher employee premiums and lower deductible exposure. Both plans include preventive care and routine vision benefits.

What is an HSA?

An HSA is a tax-advantaged account available to eligible employees enrolled in the HSA Plan.

  • Contributions are made through payroll deduction.
  • Funds in another HSA can be rolled over to consolidate.
  • The account belongs to the employee.
  • Funds in the HSA may be invested.
  • Qualified expenses include medical, dental, vision, prescription, deductible, copayment, and coinsurance expenses.

2026 limits: $4,400 for employee-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage, plus a $1,000 catch-up contribution for eligible individuals age 55 or older. Limits include employee and employer contributions.

Flexible Spending Accounts

Health Care and Limited Purpose FSAs have a $3,400 annual limit for the plan year beginning July 1, 2026. Up to $680 may roll over. The Dependent Care FSA limit is $7,500, or $3,750 if married filing separately, and has no rollover.

Employees enrolled in the HSA Plan are not eligible to enroll in a Healthcare FSA; however, they can enroll in a Limited Purpose FSA, which can be used for dental and vision expenses only.

FSA elections are made during new-hire enrollment, open enrollment, or after a qualifying event, and must be re-elected each year.

Voluntary Disability Insurance

Short-Term Disability may replace 60% of weekly pre-disability earnings, subject to plan limits and waiting periods. Long-Term Disability is intended for qualifying disabilities lasting beyond the LTD waiting period. Eligibility depends on VRS plan and service time.

Voluntary Critical Illness

Critical Illness coverage pays a lump-sum benefit upon diagnosis of a covered condition. Employee and spouse coverage are available at $10,000 or $20,000. Spouse coverage cannot exceed employee coverage. Dependent children are automatically covered at 50% of the employee election at no additional cost.

Optional Group Life Insurance

Employees may elect one to eight times their annual salary, subject to plan maximums. Spouse and child coverage are available based on the employee's election.
